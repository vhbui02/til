# Why I Have My Own Website?

From the [Positive Arguments for the Indie Web 2012](https://indieweb.org/2012/Positive_Arguments), in addition to my own experience, I've deducted my own list:

1. **Autonomy**: I control the ownership and availability of my data.
1. **Connected**: My content can be distributed in any social media platforms, their replies
1. **Web technology playground:**
   - Use my tech stacks of choice, unrestricted by the ones that were used at work. E.g. 11ty is my go-to SSG but I don't use it at work.
   - Use my own domain for my work email (both for self-hosted email server or route to Gmail)
   - Freedom to customize the looks & feels.
   - Get to design content structure.
   - SEO optimization for my static content (watch [Complete On-Page SEO Checklist by Nathen Gotch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=imsyg1wRa_Y) and [SEO for 2023: The Complete Guide](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8YDUP-RH_4g))
   - Implement pagination, searching, RSS, image transformation, ...
   - Host my own analytics software to determine who checks my website, boosts my ego and status.
   - Implement [Frontend Performance best practice checklist](https://roadmap.sh/best-practices/frontend-performance)
   - Deploy my website to one of the major cloud platforms.
1. **IndieWeb playground:** Practice
   - [Permashortlink](https://indieweb.org/permashortlink): using a URL with short-domain
   - [POSSE](https://indieweb.org/POSSE)
   - [Backfeed](https://indieweb.org/backfeed)
1. **Personal Knowledge Management (Second brain):** everything I've learned so far will be published on my website in different formats: blog posts, TILs, weekly notes in newsletter ...
1. **Advertise myself:**
   - The UI/UX + features of the website shows my professionalism.
   - Beside LinkedIn and GitHub Profile, the website itself is another weapon in my arsenal.
   - Host my own CV.
